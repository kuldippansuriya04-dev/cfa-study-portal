3
Guidance for Standards I–VII
Real situations. Real rules. Real consequences.
⚖️ Standard I — Professionalism
Know the law. Be independent & objective. No misrepresentation. No misconduct. Maintain competence.
🏦 Standard II — Capital Markets
No trading on material nonpublic information. No market manipulation. Full stop.
🤝 Standard III — Duties to Clients
Loyalty, prudence & care. Fair dealing. Suitability. Performance presentation. Confidentiality.
🏢 Standard IV — Duties to Employers
Loyalty to employer. No additional secret compensation. Proper supervision.
🔬 Standard V — Analysis & Actions
Diligence & reasonable basis. Clear communication. Keep records. No fishing without data.
🎯 Standard VI — Conflicts of Interest
Disclose conflicts. Clients before you. No referral fees without disclosure.
🎓 Standard VII — CFA Responsibilities
Don't cheat on exams. Don't misuse the CFA designation. Uphold program integrity.
Standard I(B) — Independence Alert!
"That 'gift' from a client could bias my research report. I must REFUSE or DISCLOSE!"
Never let gifts, entertainment, or pressure compromise objectivity in research.
Standard II(A) — Insider Info!
"Psst — the merger is happening tomorrow…" → DO NOT TRADE ON THIS!
Trading on inside info = illegal AND unethical. Build a firewall. Report it. Don't touch it.
Standard III — Client First!
"Loyalty, prudence, care — clients sit on the pedestal. I serve THEM first."
Duty of loyalty to clients overrides personal interests and employer pressure (within legal limits).
Standard IV — Supervisors Are Responsible Too!
When junior staff violate rules, supervisors share responsibility if they failed to implement adequate compliance systems.
"If I'm the boss and my team trades on inside info because I had no oversight — THAT'S on me!"
Supervisors must implement AND enforce adequate compliance procedures. Ignorance is not a defense.
Standard VI — Disclose Conflicts!
"If I own stock in the company I'm recommending, I MUST disclose that!"
Any personal interest that could bias advice must be disclosed — clients deserve to know.
Standard VII — Don't Cheat!
"Sharing exam questions, misusing 'CFA' on your business card — instant investigation!"
Protect the CFA designation's integrity. No cheating. No misrepresentation. Ever.
🛡️

Module 3 Key Takeaway

Standards I–VII cover every dimension of professional life — from independence and insider trading, to client duties, supervision, and protecting the CFA designation. When in doubt: disclose, dissociate, and always put clients first.

Introduction to GIPS
Global Investment Performance Standards — no more cherry-picking!
The Problem GIPS Solved
"Before GIPS, firms showed ONLY their best-performing accounts. Pure cherry-picking!"
Pre-GIPS, misleading performance data was rampant. Investors couldn't compare apples to apples.
GIPS to the Rescue!
"GIPS = global ethical standards for fair, full performance reporting. One standard for the whole world!"
GIPS promotes ethics and integrity through universal, transparent performance presentation.
The Magic of Composites
"A composite groups ALL similarly managed accounts — so you can't hide the bad ones!"
GIPS requires composites: all accounts with the same strategy go in together. No hiding losers.
Who Can Claim Compliance? Who Benefits?
Asset Managers
Can claim GIPS compliance
Investors
Can compare firms fairly
Verifiers
Independent third parties validate
"Software can't be GIPS compliant. Consultants can't claim it unless they manage actual assets. Only the FIRM claims!"
Only asset-managing firms can claim GIPS compliance. Independent verification adds extra credibility.
📊

Module 4 Key Takeaway

GIPS was created to end misleading performance presentations. It requires all similar accounts to be grouped in composites, ensures fair full disclosure, and allows investors everywhere to compare managers on equal footing. Compliance is voluntary but powerfully signals integrity.

5
Ethics Application
Real-world case studies — spot the violation!
Case 1: The Misleading Tweet
"A CEO tweets funding is 'secured' — but it was just a vague conversation. That's misrepresentation!"
Violation: Standard I(C) Misrepresentation & Standard II(B) Market Manipulation.
Case 2: New Job, New Skills Gap
"Mahajan took a new job supervising ESG analysts… but he's never supervised anyone before!"
Violation risk: Standard I(E) Competence. Must build supervisory skills before taking on the role.
Case 3: Overheard at a Party
"Khatri overheard a confidential acquisition plan. Even indirect knowledge = nonpublic material info!"
Violation: Standard II(A) Material Nonpublic Information. Mosaic theory doesn't apply here.
The Ethics Application Checklist — Use This Every Time!
In every ethics application scenario, work through the same steps:
"1. Identify the ethical issue
2. Spot relevant Standards
3. Determine if violated
4. Recommend corrective action
Always ask: What would a reasonable CFA charterholder do?"
Module 5 puts theory into practice — use the framework systematically for every scenario.
Case 4: Clients Treated Unequally
"Giving hot IPO shares ONLY to big clients = violation of Standard III(B) Fair Dealing!"
All clients must be treated fairly — not just the biggest ones. Fair dealing is non-negotiable.
The Golden Rule of Ethics
"Would I be comfortable if my boss, my client, AND the newspaper saw exactly what I just did?"
The "mirror test" — if you can't look at yourself (and the public) confidently, don't do it.
🎯

Module 5 Key Takeaway

Ethics Application bridges theory and practice. Every real-world scenario tests: Which standard applies? Was it violated? What should the professional do instead? Practice spotting violations — it's the heart of the CFA ethics exam questions.
