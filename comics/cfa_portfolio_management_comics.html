Portfolio Risk & Return — Part I
Return measures, risk measures, diversification magic, and the efficient frontier.
How Do We Measure Return?
HOLDING PERIOD RETURN
HPR = (P₁ − P₀ + D) / P₀
ARITHMETIC MEAN
RA = (r₁+r₂+…+rₙ) / n
GEOMETRIC MEAN ← USE THIS for multi-period!
RG = [(1+r₁)(1+r₂)…]^(1/n) − 1
MONEY-WEIGHTED RETURN
= IRR of all cash flows (like investor's actual return)
Geometric mean ≤ Arithmetic mean. Use geometric for past performance, arithmetic for expected return.
Variance & Standard Deviation — Measuring Risk
σ² = Σ[P(rᵢ)(rᵢ − E[r])²]
"Standard deviation is the width of the bell curve — wider = riskier!"
Variance σ² measures dispersion. Std dev σ = √σ² is in same units as return. Covariance drives portfolio risk.
Covariance & Correlation — Do They Move Together?
"ρ = −1 is the Holy Grail — perfect diversification!"
Correlation ρ ∈ [−1, +1]. Lower ρ → more diversification benefit. ρ = +1 means no diversification.
The Magic of Diversification & the Efficient Frontier!
"Every portfolio ON the frontier is optimal for its risk level. Anything below is inefficient — same risk, less return!"
MVP = Minimum Variance Portfolio. Starting point of the efficient frontier. The lowest risk you can achieve with any combination.
Diversification eliminates unsystematic risk — but systematic risk stays! Efficient frontier = best return for each risk level.
📈

Module 1 Key Takeaway

Geometric mean for multi-period returns. Variance/std dev measures risk. Correlation ρ drives diversification — lower ρ = more benefit. Portfolio variance = w²σ² + w²σ² + 2·w·w·Cov(A,B). Efficient frontier shows max return per unit of risk. MVP = lowest possible portfolio risk.

Portfolio Risk & Return — Part II
Capital Market Line, CAPM, Beta, SML, and the Sharpe Ratio.
Add a Risk-Free Asset → The Capital Market Line!
"The CML is the best possible line — risk-free rate to the Market Portfolio M!"
E[Rₚ] = Rf + [(E[Rm]−Rf)/σm] × σₚ
Systematic vs Unsystematic Risk
"Diversification ONLY removes unsystematic risk. Systematic risk stays — and that's what CAPM prices!"
Total risk = Systematic (market) + Unsystematic (firm-specific). Only systematic risk earns a return premium.
Beta β — Sensitivity to the Market
β < 0
Moves OPPOSITE to market (rare — gold, some bonds)
β = 0
No market exposure (risk-free asset)
β = 1
Moves WITH market (average stock)
β > 1
AMPLIFIED market moves (growth stocks, tech)
β = Cov(Rᵢ, Rm) / Var(Rm)
β measures systematic risk. Market portfolio β = 1 by definition. β is the relevant risk measure under CAPM.
CAPM & the Security Market Line (SML) — The Big Picture!
E[Rᵢ] = Rf + βᵢ × (E[Rm] − Rf)
Above SML → Stock is UNDERVALUED → Expected return > required → BUY

Below SML → Stock is OVERVALUED → Expected return < required → SELL
"CML uses total risk σ. SML uses systematic risk β. Different tools, same market!"
Sharpe Ratio
(Rₚ − Rf) / σₚ
Return per unit of TOTAL risk
Treynor Ratio
(Rₚ − Rf) / βₚ
Return per unit of SYSTEMATIC risk
M² (Modigliani)
Sharpe × σm + Rf
Converts Sharpe to % return
CAPM: Required return = Rf + β × Equity Risk Premium. Higher β → higher required return. Jensen's Alpha = Actual − CAPM expected.
📐

Module 2 Key Takeaway

CML = efficient portfolio line using total risk σ. SML = ALL assets using β. CAPM: E[R] = Rf + β(Rm−Rf). Above SML = undervalued, below = overvalued. Sharpe uses σ (total risk); Treynor uses β (systematic risk); Jensen's Alpha = actual − CAPM expected return.

Basics of Portfolio Planning & Construction
IPS, risk objectives, return objectives, constraints, and asset allocation.
The Investment Policy Statement (IPS) — Your Blueprint
"IPS = the contract between investor and manager. It drives EVERY decision!"
IPS documents objectives (risk, return) and constraints. It is the starting point of the portfolio management process.
TTLLU — The Five Constraints
T — Time Horizon
Short vs long horizon → risk capacity changes
T — Taxes
Tax-advantaged vs taxable accounts, tax-loss harvesting
L — Liquidity
Near-term cash needs reduce illiquid allocations
L — Legal & Regulatory
Pension funds, endowments have special rules
U — Unique Circumstances
ESG, concentrated positions, religious screens
Mnemonic: TTLLU. All five constraints shape portfolio construction just as much as return objectives.
Risk Objectives — Ability vs Willingness
"When ability and willingness conflict — use the LOWER of the two!"
Risk capacity = financial ability to bear loss. Risk tolerance = psychological willingness. The lower one dictates actual risk taken.
The Portfolio Management Process — 3 Steps!
① PLANNING
Understand client needs
IPS preparation
Capital market expectations
Strategic Asset Allocation
↓
② EXECUTION
Security analysis
Portfolio construction
Tactical asset allocation
Trade execution
↓
③ FEEDBACK
Monitor portfolio
Rebalancing
Performance measurement
Reporting to client
"It's a continuous loop — Planning feeds Execution, which feeds Feedback, which feeds back to Planning!"
Strategic AA = long-term target weights based on risk/return profile

Tactical AA = short-term deviations from SAA to exploit mispricings

Rebalancing = restoring drifted weights back to target
PM is a continuous process: Plan (IPS + objectives) → Execute (construct portfolio) → Feedback (monitor + rebalance). Repeat.
📋

Module 3 Key Takeaway

IPS = foundation of PM. Objectives = risk + return. Constraints = TTLLU (Time, Taxes, Liquidity, Legal, Unique). When risk ability and willingness conflict, use the lower. PM process: Planning → Execution → Feedback (continuous loop). Strategic AA = long-term; Tactical AA = short-term tilts.

The Behavioural Biases of Investors
Cognitive errors and emotional biases — why humans make terrible investors.
Two Types of Biases — Head vs Heart
"Cognitive errors come from flawed thinking — they can be corrected with education. Emotional biases are harder to change!"
Cognitive errors: belief perseverance + information processing errors. Emotional biases: harder to overcome, require adapting the portfolio.
Key Cognitive Biases — Belief Perseverance
CONSERVATISM
Stick to prior beliefs; underreact to new info
CONFIRMATION BIAS
Seek only info that confirms existing view
REPRESENTATIVENESS
Judge by stereotypes; classify stocks by recent performance
HINDSIGHT BIAS
"I knew it all along!" — distorts memory of past forecasts
ANCHORING
Over-rely on first piece of information (e.g. purchase price)
Also: illusion of control, mental accounting, framing. These are processing errors — correct with better data analysis.
Key Emotional Biases — Feelings > Facts
LOSS AVERSION
Losses hurt ~2× more than equal gains feel good. Hold losers too long!
OVERCONFIDENCE
Overestimate own skill; underestimate risk. Trade too much.
SELF-CONTROL BIAS
Spend too much today; fail to save for future goals
STATUS QUO BIAS
Prefer doing nothing; keep existing portfolio even if suboptimal
ENDOWMENT BIAS
Value what you OWN more than what you don't. Reluctant to sell.
Emotional biases: adapt the portfolio (widen acceptable range). Cognitive errors: educate the client. Both destroy wealth if unchecked!
Prospect Theory — Why Investors Are Irrational!
"People feel LOSSES about twice as intensely as equivalent GAINS. Reference point determines 'gain' vs 'loss'."
Disposition Effect
Sell winners too early (lock in gains)
Hold losers too long (avoid realising loss)
= Destroys after-tax returns!
Prospect theory: utility based on gains/losses from reference point, not final wealth. Loss aversion coefficient ≈ 2.25. Asymmetric pain leads to the disposition effect.
🧠

Module 4 Key Takeaway

Cognitive biases (conservatism, confirmation, representativeness, anchoring, hindsight) = faulty reasoning → correct with education. Emotional biases (loss aversion, overconfidence, status quo, endowment) = feelings → adapt the portfolio. Prospect theory: losses hurt ~2× gains feel good. Disposition effect = sell winners, hold losers.

Introduction to Risk Management
Risk governance, risk types, VaR, scenario analysis, and risk budgeting.
The Risk Universe — Know Your Enemies!
MARKET RISK
Equity, interest rate, FX, commodity price risk
CREDIT RISK
Counterparty default or credit downgrade risk
LIQUIDITY RISK
Can't sell at fair price quickly enough
OPERATIONAL RISK
Systems, people, processes, or external events fail
TAIL RISK / MODEL RISK
Extreme events; wrong model assumptions
Also: settlement risk, legal/regulatory risk, reputational risk. Risk management ≠ risk elimination. It's about UNDERSTANDING and PRICING risk.
Value at Risk (VaR) — The Famous Risk Measure
"95% VaR = $1M means: 5% chance of losing MORE than $1M over 1 day. Not the worst case!"
VaR gives a MINIMUM loss in the tail — not average tail loss (that's CVaR/ES)!
VaR limitations: doesn't capture tail severity, assumes normality, not sub-additive. CVaR (Expected Shortfall) = average loss BEYOND VaR.
Risk Governance & Risk Budgeting
"Risk budgeting allocates the total risk budget among PMs and strategies — who gets to take how much risk?"
Risk governance sets policies, limits, and oversight. Risk budget = total acceptable risk allocated across the organisation. Exceeding budget triggers review.
Scenario Analysis & Stress Testing — What If Things Go Wrong?
📊 Scenario Analysis
Applies specific macro scenarios (e.g. recession, rate hike, oil shock).
Can be historical (2008 GFC, 2020 COVID) or hypothetical.
Shows P&L under each scenario.
💥 Stress Testing
Extreme scenarios — beyond normal VaR range.
"What if equities drop 40%?" or "3σ event in rates?"
Identifies tail vulnerabilities.
"VaR tells you the threshold. Stress tests tell you what happens BEYOND that threshold. Both are needed!"
Key Risk Measures:
VaR — loss at confidence level
CVaR/ES — avg loss beyond VaR
Drawdown — peak-to-trough loss
Tracking Error — deviation from benchmark
No single risk measure is complete. Use VaR + CVaR + stress tests + scenario analysis together. Risk ≠ volatility — it includes ALL sources of potential loss.
⚠️

Module 5 Key Takeaway

Risk types: market, credit, liquidity, operational, tail/model. VaR = minimum loss in worst X% — not the average tail loss. CVaR/Expected Shortfall = average beyond VaR. Risk governance sets limits; risk budgeting allocates risk. Stress tests examine extreme scenarios beyond VaR. No single measure captures all risk.

Technical Analysis
Charts, trends, patterns, support/resistance — reading the market's footprints.
Technical vs Fundamental Analysis
📈 Technical
Price & volume data only
Past patterns predict future
Works with any asset
Short-term trading focus
Market IS the truth
💼 Fundamental
Financial statements
Intrinsic value estimate
Long-term investing
Requires judgement
Market makes mistakes
"Technicians say 'all info is in the price'. Fundamentalists say 'price can deviate from value'!"
TA assumes markets have memory — past prices contain useful info. Contradicts weak-form EMH. Used for timing, entry/exit signals.
Trends, Support & Resistance
"Support = price floor where buyers step in. Resistance = ceiling where sellers dominate. Breakout = price crosses through!"
Uptrend: higher highs + higher lows. Downtrend: lower highs + lower lows. Support/resistance reverse roles when broken.
Famous Chart Patterns
HEAD & SHOULDERS → Bearish reversal
Left shoulder, head (higher), right shoulder. Break neckline = SELL!
DOUBLE TOP / BOTTOM
Double top = bearish reversal. Double bottom = bullish reversal.
TRIANGLE (Continuation)
Ascending / Descending / Symmetrical → predicts continuation
FLAG & PENNANT (Continuation)
Brief consolidation after strong move → breakout continues
Reversal patterns = trend will change. Continuation patterns = trend will resume. Volume should confirm breakouts.
Technical Indicators — Momentum, Volume & Sentiment!
📊 Momentum / Trend
Moving Averages (MA) — SMA, EMA. Golden Cross (50 > 200 MA) = bullish!
MACD — difference of two EMAs. Signal crossover = buy/sell
RSI — 0–100. >70 = overbought, <30 = oversold
📦 Volume Indicators
On-Balance Volume (OBV) — cumulative volume. Rising with price = strong
Volume should CONFIRM price moves. Price up + low volume = weak signal!
😱 Sentiment Indicators
Put/Call Ratio — high = bearish sentiment (contrarian: buy!)
VIX — fear index. Spike = market bottom signal
Short interest — high = bearish. Contrarian = potential short squeeze
No indicator works 100%. Combine multiple signals. Oscillators (RSI, MACD) signal turning points. Trend indicators (MA) confirm direction.
📉

Module 6 Key Takeaway

TA uses price/volume to identify trends and patterns. Support = floor, resistance = ceiling — they reverse on breakout. Reversal patterns (H&S, double top/bottom) signal trend change. Continuation patterns (triangle, flag) signal trend resumption. Key indicators: MA (trend), RSI (momentum), OBV (volume), VIX/put-call (sentiment).

FinTech in Investment Management
Big data, AI/ML, robo-advisors, blockchain, and the digital transformation of finance.
Big Data — The New Oil in Finance
TRADITIONAL DATA
Financial statements, macro data, surveys
ALTERNATIVE DATA — The Edge!
Satellite imagery, social media, credit card data, web scraping, IoT sensors
3 Vs of Big Data
Volume (huge) · Variety (structured/unstructured) · Velocity (real-time)
"Counting cars in a Walmart parking lot from satellite = predicting same-store sales BEFORE earnings!"
Also: Veracity (accuracy) and Value. Alternative data must be material non-public information (MNPI) compliant — or it's illegal!
AI & Machine Learning in Investing
SUPERVISED LEARNING
Train on labelled data → predict stock returns, credit scores, fraud detection
UNSUPERVISED LEARNING
No labels → cluster stocks, detect anomalies, risk factor discovery
DEEP LEARNING / NLP
Analyse earnings call transcripts, news sentiment, 10-K filings
REINFORCEMENT LEARNING
Learn optimal trading strategy through trial and reward (like AlphaGo)
ML models can overfit historical data — garbage in, garbage out. Model risk and explainability are major concerns for regulators.
Robo-Advisors, RegTech & Distributed Ledger
🤖 Robo-Advisors
Automated portfolio management via algorithms. Low cost, passive, tax-loss harvesting. Based on questionnaire → risk profile → ETF portfolio.
⚖ RegTech
AI for regulatory compliance: AML, KYC, real-time trade surveillance, stress-test reporting.
⛓ Distributed Ledger (DLT)
Blockchain: immutable record, smart contracts for settlement, tokenised assets, faster T+0 settlement.
FinTech disrupts: trading, advisory, clearing, compliance. Key risk: cybersecurity, model risk, and regulatory uncertainty around crypto/DLT.
The FinTech Ecosystem in Asset Management
📱 WealthTech
Robo-advisors, digital onboarding, fractional investing, goal-based tools
💹 Trading Tech
HFT, algorithmic trading, smart order routing, execution algorithms (VWAP, TWAP)
🔍 Data Analytics
Alternative data, NLP on filings, machine learning alpha signals
🔐 CryptoFinance
DeFi, tokenised securities, crypto ETFs, smart contracts for OTC derivatives
"FinTech is not replacing finance — it's transforming every step of the investment process from data to execution to compliance!"
Key Exam Points:
✓ Alternative data types
✓ ML: supervised vs unsupervised
✓ Robo-advisor workflow
✓ DLT / blockchain settlement
✓ RegTech for compliance
FinTech brings efficiency, lower cost, and new data sources — but also new risks: model risk, cybersecurity, MNPI concerns, and regulatory gaps.
🤖

Module 7 Key Takeaway

Big data: traditional + alternative (satellite, social, credit card, IoT). 3 Vs: Volume, Variety, Velocity. ML types: supervised (labelled), unsupervised (clustering), deep learning/NLP, reinforcement. Robo-advisors = automated ETF portfolios. DLT/blockchain = T+0 settlement, smart contracts. RegTech = AI-driven compliance. Main risks: overfitting, MNPI, cybersecurity.
