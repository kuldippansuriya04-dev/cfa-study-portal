Fixed Income Instruments — Overview
Bond features, classifications, coupon types, and embedded options.
Anatomy of a Bond
"Lend me money, I pay coupons + return Face Value at maturity. Simple!"
Bond = debt instrument. Issuer borrows, promises periodic coupons + principal repayment. Par value typically $1,000.
Coupon Structures — Not Just Plain Vanilla!
FIXED-RATE
Coupon = fixed % of par. Predictable. E.g. 5% × $1,000 = $50/yr
FLOATING-RATE NOTE (FRN)
Coupon = Reference Rate (LIBOR/SOFR) + Spread. Resets each period.
ZERO-COUPON
No coupons! Issued at discount, matures at par. Return = price appreciation.
STEP-UP / DEFERRED
Coupon increases over time OR deferred initially then kicks in.
INFLATION-LINKED (TIPS)
Principal adjusted for CPI. Real return protected from inflation.
FRNs protect against rising rates. Zero-coupons have the highest price sensitivity. TIPS protect against inflation erosion.
Embedded Options — Strings Attached!
📞 CALLABLE BOND
Issuer can redeem early. Issued when rates high → issuer calls if rates fall & refinances cheaper. Bad for investor. Higher yield to compensate.
🤝 PUTABLE BOND
Investor can sell back to issuer at par. Good for investor → lower yield. Protects if rates rise or issuer creditworthiness falls.
🔄 CONVERTIBLE BOND
Investor can convert to equity shares. Hybrid: bond floor + equity upside. Lower yield vs straight bond.
Callable = issuer benefits → higher yield. Putable = investor benefits → lower yield. Convertible = equity kicker → lower yield. YTW = yield to worst (for callables).
Bond Universe — Who Issues What?
🏛 GOVERNMENT
Sovereign bonds (Treasuries, Gilts, JGBs)
Sub-sovereign (municipalities, agencies)
Considered risk-free (default-risk free for local currency)
🏢 CORPORATE
Investment Grade (IG): BBB-/Baa3 and above
High Yield (HY): BB+/Ba1 and below
Medium-Term Notes (MTNs), Commercial Paper
📦 STRUCTURED / ABS
Asset-Backed Securities (ABS)
Mortgage-Backed Securities (MBS)
Collateralised Debt Obligations (CDOs)
🌍 EUROBOND / FOREIGN
Eurobond: issued outside issuer's home country in foreign currency
Foreign bond: issued in domestic market by foreign entity
Seniority: Secured > Senior Unsecured > Subordinated > Equity. Higher seniority = lower yield = higher recovery in default.
💰

Module 1 Key Takeaway

Bond = par value + coupon rate + maturity + issuer + currency. Coupon types: fixed, floating (FRN), zero, inflation-linked. Embedded options: callable (issuer benefits → higher yield), putable (investor benefits → lower yield), convertible (equity kicker → lower yield). Seniority hierarchy: Secured → Senior Unsecured → Subordinated.

Fixed Income Markets — Issuance, Trading & Funding
Primary vs secondary markets, repo, money markets, and interbank lending.
Primary vs Secondary Market
"Most bonds trade OTC — dealers make markets between buyers and sellers!"
Sovereign bonds often via competitive auction. Corporate bonds via underwritten public offering or private placement. Secondary market = OTC dealer market.
Repurchase Agreement (Repo) — Short-Term Funding!
"Repo = collateralised short-term borrowing. REPO rate < fed funds rate (secured). Haircut = safety margin on collateral."
Repo: dealer sells bonds + agrees to buy back at higher price = borrowing. Reverse repo = lending. Repo rate = overnight rate. Haircut protects lender.
Money Market — Short-Term FI Instruments
T-BILLS (Government)
≤ 1 year. Issued at discount. Zero coupon. Most liquid.
COMMERCIAL PAPER (CP)
≤ 270 days. Unsecured corporate. Investment grade issuers only.
CERTIFICATES OF DEPOSIT (CD)
Bank-issued deposit receipts. Fixed maturity and interest rate.
BANKER'S ACCEPTANCE (BA)
Bank-guaranteed short-term credit. Used in trade finance.
Money market = maturities ≤ 1 year. Quoted on discount basis (T-bills, CP) or add-on yield (CDs). High liquidity, low risk.
Bond Indenture, Covenants & Legal Framework
📜 INDENTURE (Trust Deed)
The legal contract between issuer and bondholders. Sets out all terms: coupon, maturity, collateral, covenants, events of default, trustee role.
✅ AFFIRMATIVE COVENANTS
Things issuer MUST do: pay interest, maintain insurance, provide financial reports, comply with laws.
🚫 NEGATIVE COVENANTS
Things issuer CANNOT do: take on more debt (debt incurrence test), pay excessive dividends, sell key assets, merge without consent.
"Covenants protect bondholders from management acting against their interests. Violation = EVENT OF DEFAULT!"
⚡ CROSS-DEFAULT
Default on one bond = default on all. Protects junior bondholders from issuer selectively paying senior debt only.
Secured bonds → collateral pledged
Unsecured bonds → general claim on assets
Indenture = master legal document. Covenants constrain issuer behaviour. Trustee represents bondholders. Cross-default clause protects all bondholders equally.
🏦

Module 2 Key Takeaway

Primary market = new issuance (auctions, bookbuilding). Secondary market = OTC dealer market. Repo = collateralised borrowing: sell bonds today + repurchase tomorrow at higher price. Money market ≤ 1 yr: T-bills, CP, CDs, BAs. Indenture = legal contract. Covenants: affirmative (must do) + negative (must not). Cross-default protects all bondholders.

Introduction to Bond Valuation
PV of cash flows, price-yield relationship, pricing between coupon dates.
Bond Price = PV of ALL Cash Flows
P = C/(1+r) + C/(1+r)² + … + (C+FV)/(1+r)ᴺ
"Bond price = sum of PV of each coupon + PV of face value. YTM is the discount rate!"
Each cash flow discounted at YTM. Semi-annual bonds: use YTM/2 and 2N periods. Full price = flat price + accrued interest.
Price-Yield Relationship — The Inverse Law!
"Prices and yields move in OPPOSITE directions. Always. No exceptions!"
Coupon > YTM → price > par (premium). Coupon < YTM → price < par (discount). Coupon = YTM → price = par. Pull to par at maturity.
YTM, Current Yield & Flat vs Full Price
CURRENT YIELD
= Annual coupon / Market price. Simple. Ignores time value & capital gain/loss.
YIELD TO MATURITY (YTM)
IRR of all cash flows. Assumes reinvestment at YTM. Most common.
YIELD TO CALL (YTC)
IRR assuming bond called at first call date. Use for callable bonds.
FLAT vs FULL PRICE
Full (dirty) = Flat (clean) + Accrued Interest. Quote = flat; pay = full.
YTW = yield to worst = min(YTM, YTC, YTP). Always use YTW for callable/putable bonds. Accrued interest = coupon × days since last payment / days in period.
Matrix Pricing & Bond Pricing Between Coupon Dates
🔢 MATRIX PRICING
Used to estimate price of illiquid bonds. Interpolate YTM from comparable bonds with known yields. Steps:
1. Find bonds with similar maturity & credit
2. Interpolate YTM
3. Discount cash flows at interpolated YTM
📅 BETWEEN COUPON DATES
Full price = PV of cash flows at settlement
Accrued interest = coupon × (t/T)
Flat price = Full price − Accrued interest

Day count: Actual/Actual (govt), 30/360 (corp)
"You pay FULL price but the quote shows FLAT price. Accrued interest compensates the seller for the coupon period they held!"
Key relationships:
Current yield < YTM (discount bond)
Current yield > YTM (premium bond)
Current yield = YTM (par bond)
Bond price moves to par at maturity ("pull to par"). Premium bonds: price declines over time. Discount bonds: price rises over time. All else equal.
💲

Module 3 Key Takeaway

Bond price = PV of coupons + PV of face value, discounted at YTM. Price ↑ when yield ↓ (inverse relationship). Premium: coupon > YTM. Discount: coupon < YTM. Full price = flat price + accrued interest. YTW = min(YTM, YTC, YTP). Matrix pricing = interpolate from comparables. Pull to par at maturity.

Yield & Yield Spread Measures
Money market yields, BEY, G-spread, I-spread, Z-spread, OAS.
Money Market Yield Conventions
DISCOUNT BASIS (T-Bills, CP)
Yield quoted as % of FACE value. D/F × 360/t
ADD-ON YIELD (CDs, BAs)
Interest added ON TOP of principal. Interest/Principal × 360/t
BANK DISCOUNT YIELD (BDY)
= (FV−P)/FV × 360/t. US T-bill quote convention.
BOND EQUIVALENT YIELD (BEY)
Annualises semi-annual yield × 2. Standard for bond comparisons.
EFFECTIVE ANNUAL YIELD (EAY)
= (1 + periodic rate)ᴺ − 1. Accounts for compounding.
BDY understates true yield (uses face not price as base). HPY = (P₁−P₀)/P₀. BEY = 2 × semi-annual YTM. EAY = (1+BEY/2)² − 1.
Yield Spreads — Measuring Risk Premium
"Spread = compensation for credit risk, liquidity risk, and any optionality in the corporate bond!"
G-spread = yield − govt benchmark. I-spread = yield − interpolated swap rate. Z-spread = constant spread added to spot curve. OAS = Z-spread − option value.
OAS — Option-Adjusted Spread Demystified
Z-SPREAD
Constant spread over ENTIRE spot curve. For option-free bonds: OAS = Z-spread.
OAS (CALLABLE BOND)
OAS = Z-spread − Option cost. Callable → option cost is positive → OAS < Z-spread. OAS reflects CREDIT risk only.
OAS (PUTABLE BOND)
OAS = Z-spread + Option value to investor. Put option benefits investor → OAS > Z-spread.
"OAS strips out the option value — lets you compare bonds with different embedded options on equal footing!"
OAS = pure credit/liquidity spread. Use OAS to compare callable vs putable vs straight bonds. Larger OAS = more compensation for credit/liquidity risk.
Yield Curve Shapes & What They Signal!
Normal (upward sloping): Long rates > short rates. Expected economic growth + inflation over time.
Flat: Transition phase. Short ≈ long rates. Uncertainty about direction.
Inverted: Short rates > long rates. RECESSION SIGNAL! Central bank tightening.
Humped (Humped): Medium-term yields highest. Associated with peak of rate cycle.
Yield curve = term structure of interest rates. Inverted yield curve = historically reliable recession predictor. Normal = long-term growth/inflation expectations built in.
📊

Module 4 Key Takeaway

BDY (disc. basis) < BEY < EAY. G-spread = vs govt. I-spread = vs swap. Z-spread = constant spread over spot curve. OAS = Z-spread − option cost (callable) or + option value (putable). OAS = pure credit spread. Yield curve shapes: normal (growth), flat (transition), inverted (recession signal), humped (peak).

The Term Structure of Interest Rates
Spot rates, forward rates, bootstrapping, and theories of the yield curve.
Spot Rates vs Forward Rates
"Spot rate = zero-coupon yield for that maturity. Forward rate = rate implied for a FUTURE period!"
Spot rates discount single future cash flows. Forward rates: f(1,1) is 1-yr rate 1 yr from now. Derived from spot rates via no-arbitrage: (1+S₂)² = (1+S₁)×(1+f(1,1)).
Bootstrapping — Building the Spot Curve
STEP 1: 1-YEAR BOND
One CF at year 1 → S₁ = YTM of 1-yr zero. Direct!
↓
STEP 2: 2-YEAR BOND
Use S₁ to discount year-1 coupon → solve for S₂ from year-2 CF
↓
STEP 3: 3-YEAR BOND
Use S₁, S₂ → solve for S₃ from remaining year-3 CF
↓
REPEAT…
Each maturity bootstraps from previously derived spot rates. Sequential stripping!
Bootstrapping extracts spot rates from coupon bond prices. Arbitrage-free: bond price = sum of CFs discounted at maturity-matched spot rates.
Theories of the Yield Curve
PURE EXPECTATIONS THEORY
Long rates = expected future short rates. No risk premium. Inverted = market expects falling short rates.
LIQUIDITY PREFERENCE THEORY
Long rates = expected future short rates + LIQUIDITY PREMIUM. Explains why curve is usually upward sloping.
MARKET SEGMENTATION THEORY
Supply & demand in each maturity segment independently sets rates. Pension funds buy long; banks buy short.
PREFERRED HABITAT THEORY
Like segmentation but investors WILL move maturity for sufficient premium. More realistic.
Liquidity preference most widely accepted. Forward rates = expected future spot rates + liquidity premium. Preferred habitat = most flexible theory.
Arbitrage-Free Valuation — Each Cash Flow at Its Own Spot Rate!
"Under arbitrage-free valuation, each coupon is treated as a separate zero-coupon bond discounted at its OWN spot rate — not one single YTM!"
Key Relationships:
Forward rate > Spot rate (upward curve)
Forward rate < Spot rate (inverted curve)
If Yield < Spot curve → bond is RICH (overpriced)
If Yield > Spot curve → bond is CHEAP (underpriced)
Arbitrage-free value = Σ[CFₜ/(1+Sₜ)ᵗ]. If market price ≠ AF value → arbitrage opportunity. Bond stripping and reconstitution enforce no-arbitrage.
📈

Module 5 Key Takeaway

Spot rates = zero-coupon yields. Forward rates implied from spots: (1+S₂)² = (1+S₁)(1+f₁,₁). Bootstrapping builds spot curve from coupon bonds sequentially. Theories: pure expectations (no premium), liquidity preference (upward bias), market segmentation (segments independent), preferred habitat (will move for premium). Arbitrage-free valuation uses spot rates for each cash flow.

Understanding Fixed Income Risk & Return
Duration, convexity, and the full picture of bond return sources.
Duration — The Interest Rate Sensitivity Ruler
MACAULAY DURATION
Weighted avg time to receive CFs (in years). = Time to "break even" on reinvestment
MODIFIED DURATION (ModD)
= Macaulay D / (1 + YTM/m). Price sensitivity to 1% (100bps) yield change.
PRICE VALUE OF A BASIS POINT (PVBP)
= Modified Duration × Price × 0.0001. $ change for 1 bp yield move.
EFFECTIVE DURATION
For bonds with embedded options. Uses price changes in up/down rate scenarios.
%ΔP ≈ −ModD × Δy
Higher duration = more price sensitivity. Zero coupon = longest duration = most rate sensitive. Duration ↑ as maturity ↑, coupon ↓, YTM ↓.
Convexity — Duration's Error Correction!
%ΔP ≈ −ModD×Δy + ½×Convexity×(Δy)²
"Convexity is ALWAYS good! Actual price falls less and rises more than duration predicts. More convexity = better!"
Convexity = curvature of price-yield. Positive convexity: gains exceed losses for same yield change. Zero-coupons have highest convexity. Add ½×Convexity×(Δy)² to duration estimate.
Portfolio Duration & Key Rate Duration
PORTFOLIO DURATION
= Σ (wᵢ × Dᵢ). Weighted average of individual bond durations.
MONEY DURATION (Dollar Duration)
= Modified Duration × Full Price. $ change per unit change in yield.
KEY RATE DURATION (Partial Duration)
Sensitivity to a SPECIFIC maturity on the yield curve. Measures curve shape risk!
DURATION MANAGEMENT
Target duration = use futures/bonds to adjust. Add long bonds to ↑ duration.
"A 5-yr duration portfolio: if yields rise 1% → portfolio loses ~5% in value!"
Key rate duration identifies which part of the curve affects portfolio most. Useful for steepening/flattening trades. Barbell has high key rate durations at both ends.
Sources of Bond Return — The Full Picture!
3 SOURCES OF BOND RETURN
① Coupon income — periodic interest payments
② Reinvestment income — interest on reinvested coupons
③ Capital gain/loss — price change from yield moves
REINVESTMENT RISK
YTM assumes coupons reinvested at YTM. If rates fall → reinvestment rate < YTM → actual return < YTM. Zero-coupons HAVE NO reinvestment risk!
INVESTMENT HORIZON & DURATION
If horizon = Mac Duration → price risk and reinvestment risk OFFSET exactly (immunisation!)
Horizon < Duration → price risk dominates
Horizon > Duration → reinvestment risk dominates
"Immunisation: match portfolio Macaulay Duration to investment horizon — price and reinvestment risks cancel out!"
Duration Properties:
↑ Maturity → ↑ Duration
↑ Coupon → ↓ Duration
↑ YTM → ↓ Duration
Zero-coupon: D = Maturity
Callable: effective D < standard D
Horizon return = coupon + reinvest income + capital gain/loss. Duration ≈ horizon that immunises. Higher convexity = better return in all rate scenarios.
⏱️

Module 6 Key Takeaway

Macaulay duration = weighted avg time to CFs. Modified duration = price % change per 1% yield change: %ΔP ≈ −ModD × Δy. Convexity corrects for curvature: add ½×C×(Δy)². Three return sources: coupon, reinvestment, capital gain/loss. Immunisation: set Mac Duration = horizon → price risk and reinvestment risk cancel. Higher convexity = always beneficial.

Fundamentals of Credit Analysis
Credit ratings, the 4 Cs, high yield vs IG, and credit spreads.
Credit Risk — Two Components!
Expected Loss = PD × LGD × EAD
"Recovery Rate (RR) + LGD = 1. Secured bondholders recover more in bankruptcy than unsecured!"
PD = probability of missing payment. LGD = 1 − Recovery Rate. EAD = exposure at default. Secured bonds have higher recovery rates (lower LGD).
The 4 Cs of Credit Analysis
💪 CAPACITY
Ability to repay. Cash flows, revenue stability, leverage ratios (Debt/EBITDA, interest coverage).
🧭 CHARACTER
Management quality, track record, governance, willingness to repay. Tone from the top.
🏛 COLLATERAL
Assets pledged. Senior secured > unsecured > sub debt. Collateral quality matters!
📜 COVENANTS
Legal protections in indenture. Affirmative + Negative covenants limit risk-taking.
"Capacity is the MOST important C — can they actually pay?"
Also Country/Currency risk for corporate issuers. Capacity analysis = financial ratios (leverage, coverage, free cash flow). Character is qualitative.
Credit Ratings — The Alphabet Soup!
"Fallen angels = IG downgraded to HY. Rising stars = HY upgraded to IG. Both cause massive price moves!"
Moody's vs S&P vs Fitch. IG: Aaa-Baa3 / AAA-BBB-. HY: Ba1+ / BB+ and below. Rating ≠ price of bond. Notching = subsidiary rated differently from parent.
Credit Spreads, Spread Risk & High-Yield Analysis
📉 SPREAD RISK
Credit spread can widen even if govt yields unchanged → corporate bond loses value.
Spread duration ≈ modified duration for non-govt bonds.
%ΔP ≈ −Spread Duration × Δspread
📊 KEY CREDIT RATIOS
Leverage: Debt/EBITDA, Debt/Capital
Coverage: EBITDA/Interest, EBIT/Interest
Liquidity: Current ratio, Quick ratio
Free Cash Flow to Debt
🔥 HIGH YIELD SPECIFICS
HY analysis focuses more on ASSET VALUE (like equity) than IG
Liquidity, covenant analysis crucial
Capital structure = all claims (loans, bonds, mezz, equity)
Recovery in default depends on seniority
"HY spreads widen in recessions (flight to quality). IG spreads widen too but less. Spread = reward for taking credit risk!"
Structural Subordination:
Parent debt is effectively junior to subsidiary debt (even if same rating)

Special Situations:
Distressed debt → technical default
Cross-acceleration → wipes out liquidity fast
Credit spread = yield premium over risk-free. Widens in recessions, tightens in expansions. Investment grade: focus on capacity. High yield: focus on assets, covenants, liquidity.
🏦

Module 7 Key Takeaway

Credit risk = PD × LGD × EAD. Four Cs: Capacity (most important), Character, Collateral, Covenants. IG: BBB-/Baa3 and above. HY: BB+/Ba1 and below. Fallen angels = IG→HY, massive price impact. Spread duration measures credit spread sensitivity. HY analysis resembles equity analysis (asset focus). Key ratios: Debt/EBITDA, interest coverage, free cash flow.

Asset-Backed Securities (ABS)
Securitisation, MBS, prepayment risk, CMOs, and CDOs.
Securitisation — Turning Loans into Bonds!
"SPV is bankruptcy-remote — even if originator goes bust, the asset pool belongs to investors. Bankruptcy-remote isolation!"
Securitisation: originator sells loans to SPV → SPV issues tranched securities to investors. Senior gets paid first, equity tranche absorbs first losses (credit enhancement).
Mortgage-Backed Securities & Prepayment Risk
PASSTHROUGH MBS
Pool of mortgages → investors receive proportionate share of principal + interest. Monthly payments pass through.
PREPAYMENT RISK
Homeowners refinance when rates fall → cash returned early → reinvest at lower rates. Contraction risk = speeds up in falling rates.
EXTENSION RISK
When rates rise → prepayments slow → MBS extends in duration. Worst time to be stuck with low coupon!
WAM, WAC, PSA — prepayment speed measures
"MBS investors face negative convexity! Price rises less when rates fall (prepayments) and falls more when rates rise (extension)."
PSA (Public Securities Association) = standard prepayment model. 100 PSA = assumed base case. WAL (weighted average life) depends on prepayment speed assumption.
CMOs — Redirecting Cash Flows into Tranches!
SEQUENTIAL PAY CMO
All principal to Tranche A first, then B, then C. Creates short, medium, long maturity tranches from same pool.
PAC (PLANNED AMORTISATION CLASS)
PAC tranche has stable cash flows within a PSA band. Support tranche absorbs prepayment/extension variability.
Z-TRANCHE (ACCRUAL)
Receives no cash flows until all senior tranches paid. Interest accrues. Longest duration. Most extension risk.
INTEREST-ONLY (IO) & PRINCIPAL-ONLY (PO)
IO: value rises when rates rise (prepayments slow = more interest). PO: rises when rates fall (prepayments accelerate).
CMOs redistribute prepayment risk — they don't eliminate it. PAC tranches are more stable but support tranches bear concentrated prepayment risk.
Non-Mortgage ABS, CDOs & Credit Enhancement!
📦 NON-MORTGAGE ABS
Auto loan ABS — monthly principal & interest, low prepayment risk
Credit card ABS — revolving; lockout period (no principal)
Student loan ABS — government guaranteed
CMBS — commercial mortgages; balloon payments
🏗 CREDIT ENHANCEMENT
Internal: Overcollateralisation, excess spread, tranching (subordination)
External: Letter of credit, bond insurance, cash reserve fund
Senior-sub structure = most common
"Equity/junior tranche = first loss position. Takes all defaults before senior tranches are impacted. Higher yield = higher risk."
🌀 CDOs
Collateralised Debt Obligations — ABS of other bonds/loans.
CLO = CDO backed by leveraged loans.
CDO² = CDO of CDOs (played major role in 2008 crisis)
ABS vs MBS:
ABS: auto, cards, students
MBS: residential mortgages
CMBS: commercial mortgages
CMO: tranched MBS
Credit enhancement lets senior tranches achieve AAA despite lower-quality collateral. Overcollateralisation = more collateral than bonds issued. Excess spread = extra interest above what bonds pay.
🏘️

Module 8 Key Takeaway

Securitisation: originator → SPV (bankruptcy-remote) → tranched ABS. MBS: pool of mortgages; prepayment risk (contraction + extension). Negative convexity = MBS prices rise less when rates fall. CMOs redirect cash flows: sequential pay, PAC (stable), Z-tranche (accrual). Credit enhancement: internal (subordination, overcollateral) vs external (insurance, LOC). CDO = ABS of bonds; CLO = ABS of loans.
